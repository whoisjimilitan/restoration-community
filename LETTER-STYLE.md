# Letter Style: turning a raw message into Brother Jimi's morning letter

Use this every time a raw message (from /teach Version C or from Jimi directly) becomes
the letter that goes out at 6:00 AM. The goal: it reads like Jimi sat down and typed it
to one person he is mentoring. Plain, warm, direct. Never like a sermon, a blog post or an ad.

---

## 1. Hard rules (never break)

1. **Use only Jimi's ideas.** Do not add new claims, stories, examples, promises or
   personal details about Jimi. Do not invent memories, testimonies or events.
2. **One idea per letter.** If the raw message holds two ideas, keep the strongest and
   list the other in Notes as a future letter.
3. **One Scripture, quoted exactly (NIV),** with its reference. You may add one plain
   sentence of that verse's biblical setting (who said it, to whom, when) if it is
   accurate and helps the reader see why the verse fits. Flag it in Notes.
4. **Flag anything new.** Any sentence that isn't a rewording of Jimi's raw message
   (an application step, the biblical setting) is listed in Notes so Jimi can approve or cut it.
5. **Jimi approves every letter before it sends.**

---

## 2. The process (do it in this order)

1. **Name the one idea** in a single plain sentence for yourself.
   *Example: The voice you listen to most is the one you'll hear when things go wrong.*
2. **Find the reader's moment.** When in an ordinary day does this idea matter? Open there,
   usually with a question about the reader's life, not a statement about the idea.
3. **Arrange the letter in this order:**
   - **Opening:** one line that puts the reader inside their own moment.
   - **Recognition:** what it looks like in real life, so they see themselves.
   - **The truth:** what God's Word says about it, in Jimi's words.
   - **Scripture:** the proof text, with one line of setting if useful.
   - **Today:** one small, concrete thing to do today (something they can picture doing).
   - **Closing line:** echoes the opening and closes the loop. This is the shareable line.
   - **Sign-off:** "Your brother,\nJimi"
   - **P.S. (every email, word for word):** "P.S. If God spoke to you through this, or you need prayer for anything today, just hit reply and tell me."
     The one exception is the welcome letter sent after "I prayed today": its body already makes Jimi their prayer partner, so a P.S. would repeat it.
4. **Cut** any sentence that repeats another, explains what was already clear, or would
   still make sense if deleted.
5. **Read it out loud** as if across a table to one person. Rewrite any line you would not
   actually say.

---

## 3. How it must sound

- Write the way a person talks to someone he cares about: plain words, contractions
  (you're, it's, don't), short paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences.
- Speak to **one** person: "you", never "you all", "we as believers" or "people who".
- Don't assume the reader already believes. Invite, don't label: "it's for you too", not "you are now one of His disciples".
- Concrete beats abstract: "before you check your phone" beats "before anything else gets your attention".
- Warm but direct. A mentor, not a preacher, not a coach.
- Length: **120 to 200 words** in the body.

### Phrases that give writing away as generated (do not use)

- Dramatic verbs for ordinary things: *hits, rises up, lands, unlocks, fuels, anchors, shapes you*
- Abstract nouns: *input, mindset, season, journey, space, alignment, foundation* (unless Jimi used it and it fits plainly)
- Church jargon a newcomer won't know, unless explained: *murmuring, sanctification, walk in the Spirit*
- Slogan rhythms: fragment lists for drama ("Panic. Complaining. Going your own way."),
  "Not X. Y." more than once per letter, three-part lists used for effect
- Writer's devices: em dashes, colon reveals ("Here's the thing:"), "Let that sink in",
  "In a world where", "The truth is", "Here's what I know"
- Mixed metaphors: if the letter is about a voice, it ends on a voice
- Loaded or political words that put readers on the defensive: *propaganda, sheeple, the elites, woke*
- Familiar church phrases used as filler: *food for your soul, on fire for God, season of blessing*

### Never place Jimi beside God

Scripture speaks for itself. Avoid lines like "God told Joshua the same thing I'm telling you."
Jimi points to God's words; he does not stand next to them.

---

## 4. Subject line and shareable line

- **Subject:** 3 to 7 words, plain, names the idea or asks the question. No clickbait, no emoji.
- **Shareable line:** the closing line. Under 15 words, makes sense on its own, used for the
  WhatsApp share, the quote card and the inbox preview.

---

## 5. Output format

```
SUBJECT: ...
BODY:
...
Your brother,
Jimi

SHAREABLE LINE: ...
SCRIPTURE: Joshua 1:8 (NIV)
NOTES: every sentence not taken from the raw message, and any second idea held back
```

---

## 6. Final check (all must be yes)

- [ ] Every idea is Jimi's. Anything added is listed in Notes.
- [ ] One idea, one Scripture, quoted exactly.
- [ ] The first line is about the reader's life.
- [ ] There is one concrete thing to do today.
- [ ] The last line echoes the first and works on its own.
- [ ] Nothing from the "do not use" list.
- [ ] Read aloud, every line sounds like something a person would actually say.
- [ ] 120 to 200 words.

---

## 7. Worked example

**Raw message (Jimi):**
> When challenges come, the voice you've been listening to most is the voice you'll hear first.
> If you've been listening to news, fear, other opinions, those rise up. But if you've been
> engaging your heart with God's Word, if Scripture is your constant input, that's the voice
> you'll hear. Not murmuring. Not rebellion. Instead: What is God saying about this? That's the
> difference. Start today. Keep God's Word on your lips. Meditate on it day and night. Because
> when the real test comes, the foundation you've been building determines whether you panic
> or seek God's direction. (Proof text: Joshua 1:8)

**Finished letter:**

SUBJECT: The voice you'll hear first

When something goes wrong, whose voice do you hear first?

It's not always the one you say you believe. It's usually the one you've been listening to the most.

If you've been taking in the news, fear, and everyone else's opinions, that's what you'll hear. Before you know it, you're panicking, complaining, or doing things your own way.

But if you've been taking in God's Word day after day, you'll hear Him first. Instead of panicking, you'll stop and ask, "What is God saying about this?"

This is what God told Joshua just before he led Israel into the Promised Land:

"Keep this Book of the Law always on your lips; meditate on it day and night." (Joshua 1:8)

So start today. Before you check your phone, open your Bible. Take in one verse and say it out loud. Meditate on it again tonight.

Hard days will come. When they do, what you hear first will depend on what you've been listening to.

Your brother,
Jimi

SHAREABLE LINE: What you hear first will depend on what you've been listening to.
SCRIPTURE: Joshua 1:8 (NIV)
NOTES: Added the Joshua setting ("just before he led Israel into the Promised Land").
Added the concrete step ("Before you check your phone, open your Bible. Take in one verse
and say it out loud."). "Murmuring" and "rebellion" were put in plain words ("complaining", "doing things
your own way"). "Foundation" was dropped so the letter stays with one picture: the voice.

**What changed and why:**
- Opened with a question about the reader's life instead of a general statement.
- Turned the claim into something the reader recognizes in themselves.
- Put church words in plain language.
- Gave the verse its setting so it reads as proof, not repetition.
- Made "start today" a small action they can picture, tied to the verse ("on your lips" → say it out loud).
- Kept one picture throughout: what you take in is what you'll hear.
- Ended on the same picture it opened with, in a line that can be shared on its own.
