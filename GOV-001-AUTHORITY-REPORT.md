# GOV-001 Authority Report: Restoration Journey Progression Definition

## Executive Summary

The ministry documents are **clear on spiritual meaning** but **silent on technical trigger**.

**Spiritual meaning:** Progression means living faithfully with community support, not completing lessons.

**Technical trigger:** WHO initiates progression (participant, mentor, system, community) and WHEN is undefined.

This report identifies what the documents affirm and what requires ministry decision.

---

## Finding 1: What Progression Means

### What the Documents Say

**Book One, Part Five (Lines 1089-1099):**
> "The Restoration Journey is not designed to be completed as quickly as possible. It is designed to be lived. Participants are encouraged to reflect on each stage, discuss it within the community, pray through it, and apply it in everyday life. **Growth is not measured by how many lessons someone has completed. Growth is measured by the steady work of becoming a person who lives with honesty, dignity, and purpose.**"

**Book One, Part Five (Lines 1103-1121):**
> "Restoration is not always a straight path... This is not failure. It is part of continuing to live faithfully. **The Restoration Journey is not a ladder we climb once. It is a way of life we continue embracing.**"

**Book Three, Chapter Two (Lines 342-352):**
> "The platform should encourage thoughtful reflection rather than hurried activity... **Restoration grows through faithful reflection, not through simply completing tasks.**"

**Book Three, Chapter Two (Lines 384-390):**
> "The destination is not the completion of a programme. The destination is a life increasingly shaped by Jesus Christ... One faithful step at a time."

### Authority Conclusion

**✅ DEFINED: Progression means faithful participation in a way of life, NOT completion of stages.**

Progression is measured by:
- Steady work of becoming honest/dignified/purposeful
- Faithful reflection and application
- Willingness to continue the journey
- Growing alignment with Christ

**NOT measured by:**
- Number of stages completed
- Speed of completion
- Checkbox completion

## Finding 2: Role of Reflection

### What the Documents Say

**Book Three, Chapter Two (Lines 340-352):**
> "## Reflection Before Progress
> The platform should encourage thoughtful reflection rather than hurried activity. Participants should be invited to pause. To pray. To consider what God is teaching them. To respond honestly. Restoration grows through faithful reflection, not through simply completing tasks."

**Book One, Part Five (Lines 1089-1099):**
> "Participants are encouraged to reflect on each stage, discuss it within the community, pray through it, and apply it in everyday life."

### Authority Conclusion

**✅ DEFINED: Reflection is a spiritual practice, not a task.**

Reflection should:
- Encourage thoughtful engagement
- Involve prayer and honest response
- Be discussed within community
- Support faithful application in everyday life

**NOT:**
- A checkbox to unlock next stage
- Hurried activity
- Purely individual/private

## Finding 3: Community and Accountability Role

### What the Documents Say

**Book One, Part Two (Lines 387-389, 421-435):**
> "Receive accountability with humility. Offer accountability with grace...
> Accountability is one of the greatest expressions of Christian love. It is never intended to shame. It is never intended to control. Its purpose is to encourage faithfulness. Sometimes accountability celebrates progress. Sometimes it asks difficult questions. Sometimes it gently reminds us of the commitments we have made. True accountability always seeks restoration."

**Book One, Part Five (Lines 1125-1141):**
> "Although every participant is responsible for their own journey, no one is expected to walk it alone. The community walks together. Mentors walk alongside participants. Facilitators encourage growth. Members pray for one another. Together, we celebrate progress. Together, we carry burdens. Together, we continue pursuing restoration."

**Book Three, Chapter Two (Lines 356-368):**
> "Although every participant has a personal journey, no one walks alone. The platform should continually encourage connection with mentors, facilitators, and the wider community... The digital journey should always point participants back toward Christian community."

### Authority Conclusion

**✅ DEFINED: Community is essential, not optional.**

Community role:
- Celebrate progress together
- Provide accountability (celebration + gentle challenge)
- Walk alongside (not lead for them)
- Encourage faithfulness
- Strengthen trust

**✅ DEFINED: Accountability is an expression of love**, not control. Seeks restoration, not shame.

## Finding 4: Mentor Role in Progression

### What the Documents Say

**Book One, Part Six (Lines 1189-1191):**
> "A mentor is not someone who has all the answers. A mentor is someone who faithfully walks with another person, offering encouragement, accountability, wisdom, and hope."

**Book One, Part Six (Lines 1197-1217):**
> "The purpose of a mentor is simple. To encourage another person as they pursue honest work and living. Mentors remind participants that they are not alone. They listen. They encourage. They pray. They ask thoughtful questions. **They celebrate progress.** They gently encourage faithfulness during difficult seasons."

**Book One, Part Six (Lines 1275-1291):**
> "Mentors encourage. They do not control. They invite. They do not pressure. They guide. They do not manipulate. **Every participant remains responsible for their own choices. A mentor's role is to support faithful decision-making, not to make decisions for another person.**"

**Book Three, Chapter Three (Lines 440-442):**
> "They celebrate faithful progress. They remind participants that God's grace is present in every stage of restoration."

**Book Three, Chapter Three (Lines 468-480):**
> "Mentors encourage honesty. They ask caring questions. They celebrate obedience. They gently challenge participants when needed. The purpose is always restoration. Accountability should strengthen trust rather than produce fear. Participants should know they are supported, not monitored."

### Authority Conclusion

**✅ DEFINED: Mentors celebrate and support, not gate-keep.**

Mentor role:
- Celebrate progress (not approve it)
- Ask thoughtful questions
- Offer encouragement and accountability
- Support faithful decision-making
- Walk alongside, not ahead of or behind

**✅ DEFINED: Mentors do not make decisions for participants.**

Mentors should:
- Encourage without controlling
- Guide without manipulating
- Support without monitoring

**This means:** Mentors affirm and celebrate faithfulness, but the documents don't say mentors approve/gate progression.

**Book One, Part Six (Lines 1289-1291):**

## Finding 6: Progression Trigger — THE CRITICAL GAP

### What the Documents Say (Searching for trigger mechanism)

**Book One Part Five and Book Two Conclusion:** Describe progression as "taking one faithful step after another" but do NOT specify who initiates the step from stage 1 → stage 2.

**PRD 04.04:** Mentions "journey progression" in scope but does NOT define trigger mechanism.

**PRD 04.05:** Describes mentoring infrastructure but does NOT link mentors to progression approval.

**Book Three:** Says platform should "celebrate faithful progress" but does NOT say who initiates progression.

### What Is Missing

The documents define:
- ✅ What progression means (faithful living)
- ✅ Community's role (celebrate, encourage, support)
- ✅ Mentor's role (walk alongside, celebrate, guide)
- ✅ Participant's role (own their journey)
- ✅ Reflection's role (spiritual practice, not checkbox)

The documents do NOT define:
- ❌ **WHO triggers progression from stage N to stage N+1?**
  - Participant self-initiates?
  - Mentor confirms?
  - Community witnesses?
  - System automatic?
  - Hybrid model?

- ❌ **WHEN can progression happen?**
  - Immediately after reflection?
  - After waiting period?
  - After community gathering?
  - After mentor discussion?

- ❌ **WHAT criteria must be met?**
  - Reflection completion?
  - Mentor approval?
  - Community acknowledgement?
  - Participant declaration of readiness?

## Existing Constraints

### Database Already Supports Multiple Models

**StageTransition model** has fields for:
- `transitionedById` (nullable) — Who initiated/approved
- `reason` (nullable) — Why transition occurred

This structure supports:
- **Model A** (Participant-led): transitionedById = null, reason = "self-initiated"
- **Model B** (Mentor-confirmed): transitionedById = mentor_id, reason = "mentor approved"
- **Model C** (Community-witnessed): transitionedById = facilitator_id, reason = "community acknowledged"
- **Model D** (Hybrid): transitionedById = varies based on scenario

### PRD 04.05 Status

PRD 04.05 (Mentoring) is marked **DRAFT** and doesn't finalize mentor authority over progression.

If progression requires mentor approval, PRD 04.05 must be finalized first.

## Recommendation for GOVERNANCE-001

The ministry must decide ONE of these models (or an alternative):

### Option A: Participant-Led Progression with Community Support
**Philosophy:** Participant owns the journey; community celebrates it.
- Participant completes reflection
- Participant indicates readiness to continue
- System records transition (transitionedById = null)
- Community celebrates the step
- Mentor encourages without approving

**Alignment:** Honors participant responsibility; risks minimizing accountability role

---

### Option B: Mentor-Confirmed Progression
**Philosophy:** Mentor walks alongside and affirms readiness.
- Participant completes reflection
- Participant requests progression
- Mentor reviews and discusses
- Mentor affirms readiness (celebrates obedience)
- System records transition (transitionedById = mentor_id)

**Alignment:** Emphasizes mentoring; requires PRD 04.05 to be finalized first

---

### Option C: Community-Witnessed Progression
**Philosophy:** Progression is recognized in community context.
- Participant completes reflection
- Community gathering occurs (in-platform or physical)
- Community affirms the step
- System records transition (transitionedById = facilitator_id)

**Alignment:** Emphasizes community; requires gathering coordination

---

### Option D: Hybrid Model
**Philosophy:** Progression is participant-initiated but mentor-supported.
- Participant completes reflection
- Participant expresses readiness
- System marks "ready for next stage"
- Mentor discusses and affirms (without gating)
- System records transition
- Community celebrates later

**Alignment:** Balances personal responsibility with mentoring support; prepares for future mentor infrastructure
