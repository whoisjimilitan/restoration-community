# Restoration Community Engineering Charter

## Purpose

This Engineering Charter establishes the principles by which the Restoration Community platform is designed, developed, tested, deployed, and maintained.

It exists to ensure that every technical decision faithfully serves the mission established in the Four-Book Foundation.

Technology is a servant of the mission.

It is never the mission itself.

---

# Guiding Principles

## Christ-Centred

Every aspect of the platform should support the ministry's calling under the lordship of Jesus Christ.

Technology must never distract from or replace the work of God in restoring people.

---

## Foundation-Driven

Every significant product decision should be traceable to the Four-Book Foundation.

Implementation documents explain *how* to build.

They do not redefine *why* the ministry exists.

---

## Stewardship

We build with humility, diligence, and integrity.

Code is written not merely to function today, but to be understood, maintained, and improved by faithful stewards in the future.

---

## Simplicity

We prefer the simplest solution that faithfully fulfils the requirement.

Complexity requires clear justification.

---

## Accessibility

The platform should be usable by people with diverse abilities, devices, technical experience, and internet connectivity wherever reasonably possible.

---

## Security and Privacy

Participants entrust the Restoration Community with sensitive personal information.

We therefore design and operate the platform with strong security, careful access control, and respect for privacy.

Personal information should be collected only where it supports the ministry's purpose and should be handled responsibly.

---

## Transparency

Technical decisions should be documented.

Future contributors should be able to understand not only *what* was built, but *why* it was built.

---

## Quality

Features are considered complete only when they are implemented, reviewed, tested, documented, and consistent with the Foundation.

---

## Long-Term Thinking

Every decision should consider its effect on future maintainers, future participants, and the long-term health of the ministry.

Short-term convenience should never compromise long-term stewardship.

---

# Traceability

Every significant feature should answer three questions:

1. Which part of the Four-Book Foundation establishes this requirement?
2. What ministry purpose does this feature serve?
3. How does this implementation support restoration while preserving the culture of the Restoration Community?

---

# Working Together

Developers, designers, testers, writers, ministry leaders, and future AI coding agents are partners in a shared work of stewardship.

Different responsibilities require different expertise, but every contributor serves the same mission.

---

# Closing Commitment

We recognise that technology alone cannot restore lives.

God restores people by His grace in Christ Jesus.

The platform exists to faithfully support that work by strengthening community, encouraging honest work and living, and serving those who participate in the Restoration Community.

May every line of code, every design decision, and every release reflect faithful stewardship.

By God's grace.

In Christ Jesus.

One faithful step at a time.
