# /reflect — Spiritual Introspection Framework

## PURPOSE

**Goal:** Confront behavior or belief. Make listener examine themselves in the mirror.

**Outcome:** Listener knows they need to change, figures out HOW themselves.

**Energy:** Ministerial, not prescriptive. Trust Scripture and Spirit to work.

**Best for:** Short-form videos that provoke personal reflection, not comprehensive teaching.

---

## THE STRUCTURE

### 1. **Binary Title** (Confronting Choice)

Format: "X or Y?" or "X and Y?"

Examples:
- "Prayer or Panic?"
- "Sending or Sitting?"
- "The Clarity of Faith"

**Purpose:** Immediately presents a choice or tension. Makes viewer think: "Which one am I?"

**Rule:** Short, poetic, ministerial. Not a question mark, but the essence of one.

---

### 2. **The Quote** (Original, Unmediated)

Present the principle/truth as stated. Stand alone.

No explanation. No bridge. Just the revelation.

**Earns its place when:** It stands on its own without needing introduction or context.

---

### 3. **Discernment + Revelation + Benediction** (Tight, Earned)

This section has three movements, all earned:

**A) Discernment**
- Ask them to examine themselves
- Make it personal ("Are you...?" / "Do you...?")
- Ask HOW they know the difference (forces deeper thought)
- No yes/no answers — requires introspection

**B) Revelation**
- State a truth that reframes their understanding
- Can be statement, contrast, or principle
- Should land differently than the discernment question
- Examples: "When you sit still, wisdom becomes clear" / "Faith is not absence of questions, it's clarity about who you're listening to"

**C) Benediction**
- End with a wish/blessing tied to the Scripture
- Not instruction ("do this") but invocation ("may you...")
- Ties back to the principle they've just confronted
- Leaves them with assurance, not assignment

**Rule:** Every word earns its place. No filler. No explanation.

---

### 4. **Pinned Comment: Scripture Only**

One verse. Proof. No question. No CTA.

Let the Scripture speak.

**Why no question?**
- The message has already moved them emotionally/spiritually
- A question feels like manipulation
- Comments should be organic response, not prompted engagement
- Trust the Scripture and Spirit to work

---

## GUARDRAILS

✓ **No CTA** — No "comment below," "share this," "tell me what you think"
  - This is ministry, not content optimization
  - People comment if the Spirit moves them

✓ **Trust Scripture** — The verse in the pinned comment is the answer
  - Viewers who dig deeper will find it
  - Those who just watch get the message anyway

✓ **Every word earns** — No explanatory sentences
  - No "this means..." or "here's why..."
  - Let them sit with the tension

✓ **Binary titles** — "X or Y?" format, not question format
  - "Prayer or Panic?" not "Are you praying from panic?"
  - Forces choice, more confronting

✓ **No instruction** — Don't tell them what to do
  - Tell them what's true
  - Let them decide the action

✓ **Benediction over assignment** — "May you..." not "Go do..."
  - Blessing lands deeper than command
  - Respects listener agency

✓ **Personal not generic** — Make it so specific that it can't help but hit home
  - Not "some people pray from panic"
  - But "Are you someone who prays from panic?"

---

## HOW TO USE THIS FRAMEWORK

When given a quote:

1. **Read it completely** — Understand the principle at its core
2. **Choose a binary title** — What choice or tension does this present?
   - Should make viewer think immediately: "Which one am I?"
3. **Present the quote** — Unmediated, standing alone
4. **Craft discernment** — Ask them to examine themselves
   - How do you know the difference?
   - Make it personal
5. **Add revelation** — State a truth that reframes
   - Should land differently than the discernment
   - Can be a statement, contrast, or principle
6. **Close with benediction** — Wish/blessing tied to what's coming in Scripture
   - "May you..." format
   - Leaves them assured, not assigned
7. **Find Scripture** — One verse that proves the principle
   - Place in pinned comment
   - Verse only, no question, no CTA

## /reflect vs /loom

**Use /reflect when:**
- You want to CONFRONT, not teach
- Message is SHORT and PUNCHY
- You trust listeners to apply it themselves
- You want INTROSPECTION, not instruction
- Ministerial energy (not content-creator energy)

**Use /loom when:**
- You want to TEACH a principle deeply
- Message is COMPREHENSIVE with narrative arc
- You need to show them the weakness-to-weapon chain
- You want to GUIDE them to specific application
- Transformational journey (problem → solution)

Both are powerful. They serve different moments.

## QUALITY CHECKLIST

Before publishing:

✓ Title presents a clear choice or tension
✓ Quote stands alone (no bridge needed)
✓ Discernment question is personal and deep
✓ Revelation reframes without explaining
✓ Benediction ties to Scripture coming in comment
✓ Every word earns its place
✓ No CTA, no engagement prompt
✓ No instruction, only truth
✓ Scripture is one strong verse
✓ Message feels ministerial, not optimized
