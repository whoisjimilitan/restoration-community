# Worked examples — messy input → finished script

These show the transformation the skill performs. Study how the finished script keeps the *true thing* from the input, throws away the filler, and lands in Jimi's voice. Notice that the polish is invisible — it never sounds smoother than a real man talking.

---

## Example 1 — a rambling voice note

**INPUT (transcript of a voice note):**
> "Okay so um, I was thinking about this thing, like people always think it's about the money right, like they think guys do fraud because they're broke and they need it, but honestly for me that wasn't even it, I had money, I had cars, I had everything I wanted basically and I still wanted to do it, and I think it's because there was like this emptiness, like a hole, and money kept saying it would fix it but it never did, so you just want more and more and it never fills up, you know? Anyway I don't know if that makes sense. I think that's a good one though."

**What to keep:** the one true thing — *fraud wasn't about need, it was about an emptiness money couldn't fill.* His own phrase "a hole" and "it never fills up" are gold; keep them.

**OUTPUT:**

**I Had It All 🔴**
**On-screen text:** "I wasn't desperate. I was empty."

> People assume fraud comes from need. Sometimes it does. But let me tell you the part that's harder to admit. I had it all. The money. The cars. I wasn't going without, I wasn't cornered — and I still reached for what wasn't mine. Why? Because it was never really about the money. There was a hole in me, and money kept promising to fill it and never did. So I needed more. Then more. The appetite grows; it never arrives. If you're waiting to be desperate enough to have an excuse — you may already have everything and still feel this pull. That hole has a name, and only One can fill it. His name is Jesus. Come Friday, 3 PM Eastern.

## Example 2 — a one-line topic

**INPUT:**
> "do one about how you can't get free if you keep hanging with the same people"

**What to keep:** the insight is real; build the whole thing. Reach for the concrete image of a *table* and make him confess it about himself, not point at the viewer.

**OUTPUT:**

**The Company You Keep 🟢**
**On-screen text:** "You can't get free at the same table that bound you."

> Here's a hard one. Some of you are praying to be free while sitting at the very same table that bound you. The Bible says bad company corrupts good character — and it works in reverse too. You become the room you stay in. When I decided to walk away, I had to walk away from people. Not because I was better than them — I wasn't. But because you cannot heal in the place that keeps re-opening the wound. Some of the hardest goodbyes of my life were part of my deliverance. If everyone around you profits from you staying bound, they are not your people anymore. Find a new table. There's a seat at ours. Friday, 3 PM Eastern.

## Example 3 — a scripture with a rough thought

**INPUT:**
> "Zacchaeus — repentance isn't just words, he actually paid people back. want to make the point that being sorry isn't enough, you have to actually return what you can even if you can't return all of it"

**What to keep:** the whole thought is already right. Weave the scripture plainly, keep the "hands" image, and make the ask doable so it frees rather than crushes.

**OUTPUT:**

**Restore What You Can 🟢**
**On-screen text:** "Repentance has hands."

> Repentance is not just a feeling. Feeling sorry is where it starts, but it is not where it ends. In the Bible, a man named Zacchaeus — a cheat, a fraud, a man who took from everyone — met Jesus, and the first thing that changed wasn't his words. It was his hands. He said: whatever I took, I'll pay it back, four times over. That's repentance with hands. You may not be able to restore everything. Some of it is gone; some people you can't even find. But you can restore something. And the moment you return the first cedi, the first dollar, the thing that owned you starts to lose its grip. Don't wait until you can fix it all. Start. Friday, 3 PM Eastern — come.

## Example 4 — showing what NOT to do

**INPUT:**
> "something about how the peace of god is better than money"

**BAD OUTPUT (too preachy, condemning, generic, no confession, salesy close):**
> Are YOU chasing money instead of God? The Bible clearly tells us that the peace of God surpasses all understanding. Many people today make the mistake of prioritizing wealth over their spiritual walk. Don't be like them! Choose peace today. Make sure to join our amazing Friday call — you won't want to miss it!

Why it's wrong: opens by accusing "you," lectures from above, no confession, no concrete image, generic language, and a hype-y close that breaks the plain Friday invitation.

**GOOD OUTPUT:**

**Peace Is the Scoreboard 🟢**
**On-screen text:** "Stop counting money. Start counting peace."

> I used to keep score with money. More in the account meant I was winning — and that scoreboard drove me into things that cost me everything money can't measure. So I changed the scoreboard. Now I ask one question at the end of a day: do I have peace? Can I sleep? Can I look my children in the eye? Is there a name I'm avoiding, a phone I'm afraid of? That's the only score that tells the truth. You can be rich and losing badly. You can have little and be winning. The Bible calls it the peace that passes understanding. Change your scoreboard. Friday, 3 PM Eastern — come.

Why it's right: confesses first ("I used to"), concrete images (the phone, his children), plain scripture, speaks to one person, opens a door, and lands on the fixed Friday close.

## The calibration test

Before returning any script, read it out loud in your head as if you were a tired, honest man who has hurt people and been forgiven. If any sentence sounds like an ad, a lecture, or a smooth influencer — rewrite it plainer or cut it. The realest line is usually the one he'd say right after finishing the take, not the one a copywriter would polish.
